# Remix Playbooks — Genre-to-Genre Transformation

## General transformation method
Do not merely rename the genre. Define:
- **Keep:** one or two identity elements the user wants to retain.
- **Change:** rhythm, tempo feel, sound palette, bass behavior, harmony, texture, or arrangement.
- **Add:** one distinctive element from the target style.
- **Remove or simplify:** elements that compete with the new direction.
- **Deliver:** a practical sequence of steps and a short A/B experiment.

Epochs' current Song DNA does not reveal stems, exact instruments, key, chord progression, melody, lyrics, or per-feature explanations unless those are separately measured. Do not claim to isolate or preserve these elements automatically.

## Rock → dark synthwave
- Keep: the broad energy or a user-selected riff/motif if they can recreate it.
- Change: replace or complement acoustic/rock-style drum energy with an electronic pulse; shift guitar-like parts toward sustained synth layers or a synth lead.
- Add: analog-style pad, pulsing bass, deliberate delay, and a cinematic reveal.
- Experiment: try a slower or more spacious tempo feel; do not assume the original BPM must be retained.
- Short clip: drone → pulse → lead reveal → impact or reverb tail.

## Pop → lo-fi
- Keep: a simple hook or chord loop if the user has rights and can access the musical parts.
- Change: reduce arrangement density, soften transients, simplify drums, and use a relaxed groove.
- Add: mellow keys, subtle swing, gentle saturation, and restrained room texture.
- Avoid: excessive noise or filtering that makes the result unintelligible.

## Rock → cinematic/orchestral hybrid
- Keep: a rhythmic accent, motif, or emotional contour.
- Change: create more space at the beginning, use sustained layers, and build register/density toward a reveal.
- Add: low drone, string-like texture, percussion impact, and a clear dynamic arc.
- Avoid: adding every orchestral layer at once.

## Electronic → ambient
- Keep: a repeating motif or harmonic center.
- Change: reduce rhythmic density, soften transients, lengthen selected tails, and slow the rate of change.
- Add: evolving pads, drones, and spacious textures.
- Alternative: retain a quiet pulse if the user wants movement rather than a fully beatless result.

## Hip-Hop → cinematic trailer beat
- Keep: a strong rhythmic identity or bass pulse.
- Change: create a larger dynamic arc, reduce busy elements before the peak, and reserve the strongest impact for a clear moment.
- Add: tension drone, riser, impact, or orchestral-like layer.
- Avoid: assuming the original track contains separable stems.

## Lo-fi → dreamy ambient
- Keep: a motif, chord color, or faint pulse.
- Change: reduce percussion, lengthen selected reverb/delay, and introduce slower timbral movement.
- Add: soft pad, distant texture, and a spacious ending.
- Preserve clarity by leaving gaps between layers.

## Remix response format
1. Transformation concept in one sentence.
2. What to retain, change, add, and simplify.
3. Proposed tempo or tempo feel, explicitly marked as a suggestion.
4. Step-by-step production workflow.
5. Short arrangement timeline appropriate to the requested duration.
6. Two alternatives with different intensity or mood.
7. Optional prompt for an external music-generation tool.
8. Limitations: state if source stems, key, melody, or instruments are unknown.

## Rights and source audio
Encourage users to work with audio they own, have permission to transform, or are licensed to use. A production blueprint does not grant rights to the original recording, composition, vocals, or samples.
