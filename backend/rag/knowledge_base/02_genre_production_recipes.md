# Genre Production Recipes — Creator Reference

## How to use this guide
These are starting points for experimentation, not rules or guarantees. Genres overlap, tempo ranges vary, and a genre cannot be reliably identified from BPM or RMS energy alone. Adapt the recipe to the user's mood, intended use, reference material, and skill level.

## Lo-fi hip-hop
- **Common direction:** relaxed, intimate, slightly imperfect, warm.
- **Tempo starting point:** often around 65–90 BPM, or double-time interpretations around 130–180 BPM.
- **Rhythm:** soft kick and snare, restrained percussion, subtle swing, sparse fills.
- **Sound palette:** mellow keys, muted guitar, soft bass, sampled textures, room noise or vinyl-like texture used sparingly.
- **Production experiments:** gently reduce high-frequency brightness; use saturation lightly; add short room ambience; vary velocity and timing subtly.
- **Arrangement:** establish the groove quickly; introduce one texture or melodic variation every 4–8 bars; avoid excessive layering.
- **Variations:** jazzy, ambient, dusty, brighter/chill, more beat-driven.
- **Avoid assuming:** vinyl noise is mandatory, or every lo-fi track must sound muffled.

## House
- **Common direction:** steady, dance-oriented groove.
- **Tempo starting point:** roughly 118–130 BPM.
- **Rhythm:** regular four-on-the-floor kick; offbeat hats or chords; percussion supports movement.
- **Sound palette:** kick, clap/snare, hats, bass groove, chord stabs, vocal chops or atmospheric layers where appropriate.
- **Production experiments:** make kick and bass coexist through note choice, envelope, EQ, and sidechain compression if desired; automate filters for transitions.
- **Arrangement:** introduce elements gradually; use breakdowns and returns to create contrast.
- **Variations:** deep, soulful, tech, melodic, progressive, minimal.
- **Avoid assuming:** every house track needs sidechain pumping or the same chord voicing.

## Techno
- **Common direction:** repetitive, hypnotic, groove- or texture-focused.
- **Tempo starting point:** roughly 120–140 BPM, with substantial variation across substyles.
- **Rhythm:** persistent kick, hats and percussion; evolution through timbre, density, and automation.
- **Sound palette:** drum-machine hits, industrial textures, drones, sequenced bass or synth patterns.
- **Production experiments:** automate filter cutoff, resonance, distortion, reverb sends, or rhythmic layers over time.
- **Arrangement:** repetition with gradual change can be more effective than constant new melodies.
- **Variations:** minimal, melodic, industrial, dub, hard techno.

## Synthwave
- **Common direction:** retro-futuristic, cinematic, nostalgic, nocturnal.
- **Tempo starting point:** often around 80–120 BPM, depending on whether the feel is slow and cinematic or driving.
- **Rhythm:** electronic kick/snare, gated or roomy snare character, steady bass pulse, arpeggiated patterns where suitable.
- **Sound palette:** analog-style pads, bright synth leads, arpeggios, chorus-treated textures, sustained bass.
- **Production experiments:** layer a simple pad with a more defined lead; use chorus or modulation for width; use delay/reverb deliberately so the mix stays clear.
- **Arrangement:** establish a mood, introduce a pulse, then reveal the main lead or motif.
- **Variations:** dreamy, dark, outrun, minimal, cinematic.

## Ambient
- **Common direction:** atmosphere, space, slow development; rhythm may be absent or understated.
- **Tempo starting point:** tempo may be irrelevant; if a pulse is used, try a slow or moderate one and judge by feel.
- **Sound palette:** pads, drones, processed field recordings, long textures, sparse melodic fragments.
- **Production experiments:** long reverb tails, slow modulation, granular or reversed textures, gradual filter movement.
- **Arrangement:** evolve timbre and density slowly; allow space and silence.
- **Avoid assuming:** ambient means no rhythm or no melody.

## Phonk
- **Common direction:** bass-forward, gritty, rhythmic; the term covers multiple related styles.
- **Tempo starting point:** vary by substyle; experiment around 90–115 BPM or use double-time interpretations.
- **Rhythm:** punchy kick, snare/clap, busy hats or rolls, syncopated patterns.
- **Sound palette:** distorted or saturated bass, cowbell-like melodic motifs in some styles, chopped vocal textures where legally permitted.
- **Production experiments:** use distortion in stages; keep kick transients readable; compare a clean low-end layer with a distorted mid-bass layer.
- **Arrangement:** make the groove clear early; use drops, fills, and abrupt transitions intentionally.
- **Avoid assuming:** all phonk shares one sound or requires a specific sample.

## Drum & bass
- **Common direction:** fast breakbeat energy with detailed drums and bass movement.
- **Tempo starting point:** commonly around 160–180 BPM.
- **Rhythm:** breakbeat or programmed syncopation, strong snare placement, active hats and ghost notes.
- **Sound palette:** sub-bass, reese-style bass, breakbeats, pads or stabs depending on substyle.
- **Production experiments:** separate sub and mid-bass roles; check mono compatibility of low frequencies; keep drum transients defined.
- **Arrangement:** alternate dense sections with breakdowns or sparse passages to avoid fatigue.

## Rock and metal
- **Common direction:** guitar-led energy, live or programmed drums, bass supporting the riff and kick.
- **Tempo starting point:** broad and subgenre-dependent; choose tempo to support riff phrasing rather than forcing a generic range.
- **Sound palette:** guitars, bass, drums, vocals or instrumental leads.
- **Production experiments:** compare doubled rhythm guitars with a single centered part; check bass/guitar masking; use automation to distinguish sections.
- **Arrangement:** riff, verse-like section, lift, chorus/drop or solo section; short-form tracks may go straight to the signature riff.
- **Avoid assuming:** all rock needs distorted guitars or a conventional verse-chorus structure.

## Cinematic/orchestral hybrid
- **Common direction:** emotional scale, tension, impact, or a sense of reveal.
- **Sound palette:** sustained strings, low drones, brass-like layers, percussion, piano, synthetic textures.
- **Production experiments:** build from sparse low-frequency tension to a wider upper-register layer; use impacts sparingly; automate dynamics rather than making every layer loud.
- **Arrangement:** establish a motif, build tension, deliver a reveal, then leave a tail or resolve.
- **Short-form tip:** the main emotional moment should arrive before the clip ends.

## Cross-genre design
To combine styles, select one primary identity and one supporting influence. Example: “ambient + trap drums” means spacious pads and long tails with a restrained trap rhythm; it does not mean stacking every convention from both genres.
