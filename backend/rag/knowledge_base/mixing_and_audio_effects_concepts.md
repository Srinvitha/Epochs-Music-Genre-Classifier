# Mixing and Audio Effects: Practical Concepts

## Scope and provenance
Original general notes on audio effects and workflow. REAPER's official User Guide and ReaEffects Guide are linked as further reading, but these notes do not reproduce those manuals.

## Equalization
- EQ changes the level of selected frequency regions.
- Use it to address masking, unwanted resonances or unnecessary low-frequency content; do not apply generic cuts without listening.
- Mute or simplify competing parts before trying to fix every issue with EQ.
- Compare processed and unprocessed versions at similar loudness.

## Compression
- Compression reduces dynamic range when a signal crosses a threshold, with behavior controlled by ratio, attack, release and related parameters.
- Attack affects how much of a transient passes before gain reduction; release affects how quickly gain reduction recovers.
- Settings depend on source material and intended dynamics. There is no single correct setting for every source or genre.

## Reverb and delay
- Reverb adds a sense of space and decay; delay creates distinct repeats.
- Long tails may create atmosphere but can obscure rapid events or crowd a short clip.
- Automation can add space during transitions while keeping the groove clear elsewhere.

## Distortion and saturation
- These effects add harmonics and can increase perceived density.
- Heavy processing can make transients less distinct or produce harshness.
- Parallel processing can blend a processed signal with a clearer version, if the DAW supports it.

## Troubleshooting flow
1. Identify the symptom: muddy, harsh, weak low end, flat, or too busy.
2. Mute layers to find contributors.
3. Check arrangement and frequency/rhythm overlap.
4. Try one processing change.
5. Match loudness and compare before/after.
6. Keep the change only if it improves the intended result.
