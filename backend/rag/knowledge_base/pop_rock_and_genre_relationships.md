## Useful distinctions
- A style label can refer to rhythmic feel, instrumentation, harmonic vocabulary, performance practice, production aesthetic or cultural scene.
- A track can combine conventions from several styles. A genre name is a creative brief, not a strict formula.
- A familiar chord progression may appear across many genres. Rhythm, sound palette, phrasing and arrangement can help distinguish the result.
- “Instrumental” describes vocal/format properties more than one unified genre. “International” is a broad dataset category, not a single coherent production style.

## Building a hybrid style
To create a hybrid, choose:
1. One primary identity (for example, a steady electronic groove).
2. One supporting influence (for example, spacious ambient textures).
3. One recognizable element to retain (motif, pulse, chord color or timbre).
4. One element to simplify or replace.
5. A deliberate transition or ending.

Avoid stacking every convention from both genres. A clear primary identity usually makes a short cue easier to understand.

## Epochs classifier boundary
Epochs' current FMA Small classifier predicts eight broad labels: Electronic, Experimental, Folk, Hip-Hop, Instrumental, International, Pop, and Rock. Production notes for synthwave, house or ambient do not mean the current model can classify those subgenres.
