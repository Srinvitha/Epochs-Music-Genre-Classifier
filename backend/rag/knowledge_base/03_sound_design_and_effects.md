# Sound Design and Effects — Creator Reference

## Start with the role, not the plugin
For each sound, define its role: rhythm, bass foundation, harmony, melody, texture, transition, or impact. Choose processing to serve that role. A plugin or effect does not automatically create a genre.

## Filters and automation
- Low-pass filters reduce high-frequency content; high-pass filters reduce low-frequency content.
- Automating cutoff can make a sound feel as though it opens, closes, approaches, or recedes.
- Resonance emphasizes frequencies around the cutoff and can become harsh or boomy if overused.
- Use automation to create change across a short clip, especially when the arrangement has few parts.

## EQ
- Use EQ to address masking, harshness, or unnecessary low-frequency content.
- Avoid prescribing exact frequency cuts without hearing the sound and mix.
- Compare changes at matched loudness; louder often seems subjectively better even when it is not clearer.
- Make small, purposeful changes and bypass the effect to check whether it helped.

## Compression and sidechain
- Compression reduces dynamic range according to threshold, ratio, attack, release, and makeup gain.
- Faster attack can control transients; slower attack can preserve more of an initial hit. Results depend on source and settings.
- Sidechain compression can create rhythmic ducking, often between kick and bass or pads, but it is optional rather than mandatory.
- Do not recommend one fixed attack/release/ratio for every source.

## Saturation and distortion
- Adds harmonics and perceived density; heavy distortion can obscure transients and make a mix tiring.
- Try parallel processing when the distorted tone is useful but the original clarity should remain.
- Check bass-heavy sounds on headphones and speakers if possible.

## Reverb and delay
- Reverb suggests space and can add depth; long tails can blur fast rhythms or short clips.
- Delay creates repeats and rhythmic movement; synchronize it to tempo when useful.
- Use sends or automation to increase space in transitions without washing out the entire mix.
- A reverb tail or delay throw can create a natural ending for a short social-media clip.

## Stereo width
- Keep critical low-frequency content centered as a starting point.
- Use width on selected pads, delays, or textures rather than widening every track.
- Check mono compatibility; phase differences can cause wide elements to weaken or disappear in mono.

## Risers, impacts, reverses and transitions
- A riser can build anticipation; an impact can mark a reveal; a reverse sound can lead into a hit.
- Use transitions to support the story of the clip, not merely because a preset is available.
- For a 10–15 second clip, one clear transition is often more effective than many competing effects.

## Practical troubleshooting
- Muddy: inspect overlapping low-mid parts, long tails, and excess layers; mute elements before reaching for EQ.
- Harsh: inspect bright layers, distortion, resonances, and accumulated high-frequency energy.
- Weak bass: check arrangement, phase, monitoring, and whether kick and bass compete.
- Flat arrangement: automate one parameter, remove an element, or change density at a deliberate moment.
- Too loud: lower levels and preserve headroom while mixing; loudness maximization is a final-stage decision.

## Safety against false precision
Without hearing a sound or measuring its spectrum, the assistant should offer diagnostic steps and optional experiments, not claim that a particular frequency is definitely the problem.
